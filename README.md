# Perchlings

Cute 3D pets that live on your desktop. You buy one, download it, click it, and it hops out and wanders your screen. Close the window; the pet stays. Name it, unlock the tricks you like, dress it from its closet. It learns your routine, celebrates birthdays, and sulks when you ignore it.

This repo is the whole product: the pets' look, the tools that turn that look into game-ready art, the desktop app, and the shop site.

## Layout

```
web/pets.js                 The pets and their closet items, as three.js geometry. Single source of truth for the look.
web/look-test.html          Interactive demo (drag to turn, click a pet, pick a mood and a hat). Loads pets.js.
web/dist/look-test.html     Same demo with pets.js inlined, for publishing. Built by tools/build_looktest.py.
tools/sprites/sprite.html   Renders one pet, one mood, one pose, one angle, transparent background.
tools/sprites/render_sprites.py   Drives headless Chrome over every pet/mood/pose/angle and packs sprite sheets.
assets/sprites/<pet>/       Rendered frames + <pet>_sheet.png + <pet>_sheet.json (generated, not hand-edited).
assets/sprites/<pet>/outfits/   One sheet per closet item, rendered as a see-through layer that fits that pet.
docs/                       Look recipe, catalog, decisions, house style for words.
app/perchling.py            The Windows desktop app: one pet, always on top, wandering the taskbar.
app/species/<pet>.json      What each pet can do: its catalog of tricks and behaviours, and its signature move.
app/closet.json             The closet: shelves and items, by id. Geometry for each item is in web/pets.js.
Perchling.bat               Double-click to start your adopted pets (first time: the adoption window). "Perchling.bat horns" starts Gold.
tools/build_exe.py          Builds the download: dist/Perchlings/Perchlings.exe and a zip. Nothing in dist/ or build/ is committed.
site/                       The shop site, plain HTML. Published to GitHub Pages by .github/workflows/pages.yml.
site/pets.js, site/img/     Generated by tools/build_site.py from web/pets.js and the sprite sheets. Do not edit by hand.
```

## Run things

Needs Python 3.12 with Pillow, and Chrome or Edge installed. No admin rights needed.

```
python app/perchling.py --pet antenna               # put Teal on the desktop (ears, leaf, horns for the others)
python tools/build_looktest.py                      # rebuild web/dist/look-test.html
python tools/sprites/render_sprites.py --pets antenna --moods happy,surprised,sleepy,sulky --poses idle,blink,walk1,walk2,squash,stretch --yaws 0,60,300 --shadow 0
python tools/sprites/render_sprites.py --pets antenna --layers beanie,crown --poses idle,walk1,walk2,squash,stretch --yaws 0,60,300 --shadow 0
python tools/sprites/render_sprites.py --pets leaf --layers beanie --yaws 0 --keep     # pet and hat together, to check the fit
```

Open `web/dist/look-test.html` in a browser to see the pets.

## How the desktop app draws them

Real-time 3D on the desktop is v2. v1 pre-renders the same three.js pets to transparent sprite frames (this repo's `tools/sprites`) and plays them in a small always-on-top window, the same way Beam works. At pet size it looks the same as live 3D and costs almost nothing to run.

Start it with no arguments and it opens the household window the first time (a code, which pet, its name), then starts every adopted pet and the house after that. Right-click the pet for its panel, a card with everything on it: Do (Tickle, Tricks, Together, Play with the others, Dance, Hide, Bathroom break, Nap, Go inside), Say and share (Notebook, Remind me, Hold a sign, Photo, Clip 8 seconds, Throw a party), Wear and own (Closet, Hat maker, Shop, Enter a code, Picks), Household (Pets, the house, Egg, Streamer stage) and Settings (Attitude, Music, Reacts, With Windows, Size, Rename, Your birthday, Let go), with Update and Quit in the footer. "With Windows" writes one small .cmd file into the owner's own Startup folder, so the pet is there at every login without admin rights. What it learns (login times, attention, what it wears) is saved in `%APPDATA%\Perchlings\<pet>.json` and nowhere else.

Lonely means: no cursor on the pet for 1 hour; from then on it hops and asks "Play with me?" every 5 to 7 minutes. Ignored means: no cursor on the pet for 2 hours. Hovering, clicking, dragging, tickling and opening its menu all count as touching (`last_touch` in the state file). After 2 hours it sulks (back turned, "Hmph.", a glance over the shoulder now and then) and keeps sulking until touched; a tickle forgives it at once. Everything it does on its own (tricks, sitting, breaks, the activities it starts) carries on regardless.

Monitors: drag the pet onto any screen and let go; it falls to that screen's taskbar and remembers the screen for next time (`mon` in the state file).

## The closet

Items are built in `web/pets.js` next to the pets, hung off the head so they follow every pose, and seated per pet so the pet's own feature stays in view (a hat sits to one side on Teal so the antenna is clear, to the other side on Green for the leaf). Each item is rendered as its own sprite sheet per pet, with the pet blocking the view where it should but painting nothing. The app lays the item frame over the pet frame, so one pet sheet works with any number of items.

Hats first, because they are the shelf people buy most (`docs/catalog.md`): beanie, crown, party hat, chef hat, mushroom cap, flower crown. Then the face shelf (glasses, sunglasses) and headphones. One item per shelf can be worn at once, and they stack: glasses under headphones under a hat. Shelves still to come, in order of evidence: neck, body, back, glow, colours.

## The download

```
python tools/build_exe.py                           # dist/Perchlings/ and dist/Perchlings-<version>-windows.zip
dist\Perchlings\Perchlings.exe --pet antenna --selftest
```

`tools/installer.iss` turns that folder into `PerchlingsSetup-<version>.exe` with Inno Setup, and into one flavour per pet (`-Teal`, `-Pink`, `-Green`, `-Gold`) that leaves a `pet.txt` next to the exe so the bought pet walks out with no questions asked; the plain one asks. Details: installs for the current user only (no admin password), desktop icon and Start menu entry, optional "start with Windows", shows up in Add or remove programs, and opens the pet when it finishes. GitHub's Windows machine has Inno Setup already, so the release carries both the installer and the zip.

GitHub builds it too: `.github/workflows/build-download.yml` runs the same script on a Windows machine at GitHub every time `app/`, the sheets or the build script change, and attaches the zip to a release named after `VERSION` (https://github.com/Pinkpelara/perchling/releases). The publish step gets three tries of five minutes each (GitHub once dropped the fourth upload with "Error saving asset", which is how 0.28.1 never went out, and once left it hanging for twenty minutes), and a last step fails the run unless all four files are on the release. The local build is for testing before a push.

One folder, one exe, about 39 MB zipped. It ships the app, the species packs, the closet and the packed sprite sheets, nothing else. The zip also carries a short READ ME FIRST.txt. It is not code-signed yet, so Windows shows its "unknown app" warning on first run; the Microsoft Store listing is what removes that. The version number lives in `app/perchling.py` (`VERSION`) and shows at the bottom of the pet's menu.

## The notebook

`app/petnotes.py`. The owner types notes into the pet (menu: Notebook...; `notes` in the state file, up to 300 of 240 characters). The pet reacts on save, brings a note up every 15 to 35 minutes when things are calm (sooner right after a new note), mentions the latest one on arrival half the time, and passes names and likes on in gossip. Recall is pattern matching, nothing more: a named person or pet ("How's Max?"), likes and dislikes, a favorite, a feeling ("Better today?"), an event (before: "Ready for the exam?"; two days on: "How did the exam go?"), something said about the pet ("You said I'm shy."), a plan ("You're learning to play guitar, right?"); anything else comes back as "You told me yesterday: ...". Never sent anywhere. The owner's name and pronouns ("my name is Polin", "my name is polin", "call me Sam", "I'm a woman", "im a girl", "I am a she", "she/her", "they/them") are learned from any pet's notebook (`owner_from_notes`; a lowercase name is capitalized) and kept in `%APPDATA%\Perchlings\owner.json` for the whole household; a pet or person named in any case after "is called", "is named" or "named" counts too (`person_named`), while "my dog Max" needs the capital; lines to the owner then carry the name ("Right on time, Polin.") and gossip uses the name and pronouns, every other line.

## What they say at the table

`app/pettalk.py`. The gossip is about the owner first: every note is used (the ones no rule understands are quoted as "{Owner} told me: ..."), plus what people write about where they live, where they work, what they want and what they own (`petnotes.RULES` grew the same patterns for recall), plus the day's memory from every pet (the most tickles, the trick asked for most, a typing burst, minutes away); the routine (usual time, hours since a touch), the picks, the adoption days and "we don't know your name" are filler that only comes out when there's nothing else. With a name known, the second reference uses the pronoun (`Owner.pro`, `Owner.pv`). `app/pettalk.py`. The pet that calls a gossip reads every pet's file (`household_states()`), pulls the facts (`facts()`: people and pets by name, likes, dislikes, favorites, feelings, events with "on Friday" turned into days from now, plans, what the owner said about each pet, picks, days since adoption, hours since each pet was touched, birthday and days until it, the usual arrival time and whether today was early or late, the next reminder) and writes one conversation (`conversation()`): up to six short exchanges that follow on from each other ("Polin has an interview coming up." / "In 4 days." / "Let's be quiet that morning."), lines about a particular pet said by that pet, seats rotating otherwise. The whole script travels in the plan file, so every pet at the table follows the same one. There is no filler; a household with nothing written down talks about arrival times, who was touched last, picks, and the fact that nobody knows the owner's name yet.

## Two pets on one desktop

`app/household.py`. Each pet writes where it is to `%APPDATA%\Perchlings\household\here\<pet>.json` a few times a second and reads the others. Now and then (every 4 to 12 minutes, when both are idle on the same screen) one writes a plan (`plans\<a>-<b>.json`: kind, shared start time, meeting point, seed); the other picks it up within half a second, and both build their own side of the same script from the same seed, so they stay in step without talking again. Pair kinds: dance, chase, wrestle, race, nap, copycat, hatswap, peekaboo, gossip. With three or more out, a plan can include everyone (`group`, slots in a line): dance, race, nap, peekaboo, parade (lines come from what each pet knows: usual login time, how long since a touch, birthday, the other's hat). A pet that comes out gets a wave. The owner can ask for any of these from the menu ("Play with the others"; the nearest pet, or everyone for the group kinds). After a play everyone walks off in a different direction, and idle walks are sometimes long strolls, so they don't end up clumped. Nothing leaves the computer.

## Music, reactions, mischief, signs, photos, parties

`app/fun.py`. `AudioEar` reads the speakers' peak level ten times a second (pycaw + comtypes, Core Audio meter; no audio content, just loudness) and sets `music` after 2.5 s of sound; a pet that is idle then dances (squash/stretch on a beat, turning left and right, a small hop) until it's been quiet for 4 s. `KeyWatch` polls key states with GetAsyncKeyState and keeps only timestamps: typing rate, click rate, Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+S runs; never which keys. Reactions (menu toggle "Reacts to you"): typing ≥5 keys/s → a hop and "Go go go." (once per 10 min), 3 undos in 6 s → "Oops.", 3 saves → "Saved. Again.", ≥4 clicks/s → "Easy.", the lock screen (OpenInputDesktop fails) → sleep until unlocked, then "Welcome back.", midnight → a yawn. Mischief is a habit pick: every 8 to 20 minutes one of: hold the cursor on the pet for 1.6 s ("Mine."), a walk that leaves muddy footprints (small colour-keyed windows that vanish after 9 s), or dragging a sticky note ("be nice to Pip") that stays for 30 s. "Hold a sign..." draws the words on a board the pet holds for 30 s or until clicked. "Photo..." saves a framed card with stickers to Pictures\Perchlings and opens the folder. Parties: on a birthday or adoption day, confetti (an 18-frame overlay) and a party hat for 3 minutes; `household/party.json` gets every other pet to join.

AudioEar judges sound over a window (music on when 60% of the last three seconds had level above 0.008, off after four seconds with almost none), because music at low volume dips under any bar between beats; the Music tile on the panel says "hears sound" while it does. A dance runs in sets of one to two and a half minutes; when the music goes on past a set, the pet takes a breather of 40 to 100 seconds (`dance_rest_until`) and does its other things, then dances again, still on the shared clock. KeyWatch (`fun.py`) is a thread polling `GetAsyncKeyState` for the typing keys every 30 ms while the keyboard is busy (100 ms when quiet), counting key-down moments only. Polling every 100 ms from the tick saw a third of real presses (a tap lasts 40 to 80 ms), and the "pressed since last call" flag is shared between processes, so pets would eat each other's presses. Bars: 4 keys/s over four seconds for the cheer (once per 4 minutes), three clicks/s for "Easy." (once per 5), three Ctrl+Z (once per 3) or Ctrl+S (once per 5) within six seconds. Reactions are a household event: every pet counts keys itself, but the first to notice a burst writes it to `household/react.json` and the others join in from `mind_others()` (`follow_reaction`), and the cooldown per kind lives in that file (`REACT_COOLDOWN`), so one burst gets a line from every pet that is free at the same moment and none fires again until the household's cooldown is over. Free means (`reactive()`): the toggle is on and the pet is idle, walking, sitting, dancing, chasing, or mid-trick it started itself; dancing, it says the line without the hop; asleep, in the house, behind the curtain, hiding, in a play (`play_until`) or in your hand it stays quiet. The hover-and-leave bit is deterministic and every character has one (`leave_bit`: the diva faints, the menace gives a look, the snoop ducks, the slacker flops for 2.6 s with its rot line), every time the pet is free, at most once per 90 s. The dance schedule is shared (`dance_slot`: 150 s on, 30 s off, by the wall clock), so no pet dances while the others rest. The owner's birthday is household-wide: `set_birthday` writes it to `owner.json` and into every pet's file, and `arrive()` copies it to a pet that doesn't have it. **Reactions always happen, and fast (0.28.0).** `reactive()` answers "full", "say" or None instead of a boolean: in full mode the pet drops whatever it is doing on its own (`interrupt()`: the trick, the stroll, the nap, the chase) and reacts with its body; in say mode (dancing, keeping you company, holding a sign, sulking, in a play) it says the line and stays put; None only when it can't be seen (hidden, inside, behind the curtain, in your hand). The bars are lower and quicker (7 keys in 2 s, 5 clicks in 2 s, 3 undos in 6 s, 3 saves in 8 s), the household cooldowns are a minute or less (`REACT_COOLDOWN`), and a burst during the cooldown still gets a nod (`react_to("notice")`: one squash, no line, at most every `NOTICE_EVERY` seconds) so nothing goes unanswered. Away and back: `away_watch()` reads the PC's idle clock (`fun.idle_seconds`, GetLastInputInfo) and the lock screen; after `AWAY_AFTER` (3 min) the pet looks around and sits by the door (`_sit_and_wait`, no wandering, no mischief), after `AWAY_NAP_AFTER` (12 min) it naps, and the first tap or click brings `come_back()`: up, a stretch, hello with the owner's name, broadcast as kind "back" so every pet says hi at the same moment; the lock screen is the same path with a nap right away. The cursor: landing on the pet gets a look up at once (`notice_until`), and while idle the pet turns its head toward a cursor within 320 px (`face_x`). Attention: a nudge after `LONELY_AFTER` (20 min) and a sulk after `IGNORED_AFTER` (60 min), and time away doesn't count as ignoring. The owner's commands always go through: `ready()` ends whatever the pet is doing (out of the folder, out of the house, up from keeping you company, curtain up, dance over, awake) before a trick, a nap, a dance, a party, a play, a break or the house, so nothing on the menu ever answers "In a minute." A day's memory (`remember_today`: tickles, tricks asked for, cheers, minutes away, notes) lives in `st["today"]` and feeds the gossip. **Real life, mid-errand (0.28.3).** The walk to the house door (Go inside, Nap in the bedroom) is an errand the owner asked for, kept in `self.errand` as (room, seconds): a typing burst, a tickle or a drag during it plays out with the whole body, the way anything else does, and then the pet carries on to the door from wherever it is (`set_aside_errand()` before the interruption, `carry_on()` after the reaction's routine is queued, which calls `go_inside(..., resume=True)` again so the walk is recomputed from the new spot and the line just said stays up). Before that, the reaction's `interrupt()` dropped the routine and its `after_routine`, and the pet forgot it was going inside whenever the owner typed fast at the wrong moment. The cursor landing on the pet mid-move gets the same glance up as when it is idle (`_run_routine` swaps in the surprised face for the poses the sheet has it for, `notice_until`), without breaking the move. **Audit fixes (0.28.1).** A pet whose own detection of a burst came a tick before its household check only nodded and never joined the other pet's reaction (the nod stamped `react_seen`); now `reactions()` joins a fresh broadcast of the same kind before it falls back to a nod, and a nod doesn't touch `react_seen`. A panel's 60 ms focus timer and its focus-out timer outlived a quickly closed window and fired into whatever Tk command had reused the name (a TypeError in the log); both panels keep their timer ids and cancel them in `close()`. The pet's panel bound the mouse wheel globally and unbound it globally on close, wiping the shop dialog's scrolling; it binds on its own window now. State files are written atomically (`write_json_safely`: tmp, a `.json.bak` copy of the last good file, `os.replace`) and read with a fallback to the copy (`read_json_safely`, used by `load_state`, `pet_state`, `owner_file`), so a crash mid-write can't take a pet down; `let_go` deletes the copy too; `react.json` is written atomically. A reminder due while the pet was inside the house was said by a bubble with no pet; `deliver_reminders` goes through `ready()` now. The Reacts and Music toggles are household switches too (`Pet.flag(key)` reads `owner.json`, cached 1.5 s; `set_flag` writes it; both default on): one tile on any pet's panel applies to every pet, so no pet is quietly left out the way a per-pet toggle could leave it, and the panel header says "reactions off" or "music off" while a switch is off.

## What the cursor does to it (0.29.0)

Three things the mouse does, all on the panel too (Do > Cursor tricks, with the gesture in each tile's tip). **Picked up**, the pet shows `surprised_dangle1/2` (new poses in `pets.js` `setPose`: a little stretched, legs kicking, arms out, looking down; rendered at yaw 0 for happy and surprised). After `CHUTE_AFTER` (1.2 s) in the hand, or lifted `CHUTE_LIFT` (60 px) above where it was picked up, the parachute comes out: `open_chute()` makes a `fun.Overlay` twice the pet's width from `assets/sprites/_props/parachute_sheet.png` (three sways L, C, R, a real three.js canopy from `pets.js` `makeParachute`, rendered alone by `sprite.html?prop=parachute` and packed by `render_sprites.py --prop parachute`), its lines meeting at the ring just above the head; `chute_tick()` keeps it over the pet and sways it. Let go up high (over 30 px) with the chute out and the pet floats (`start_float`, state `float`: 3 px a tick down, a sine drift sideways, `happy_dangle*`, a `lifted` line), lands with a squash and a `land` line, and the chute goes away; let go low, or before the chute, and it drops the way it always did. `parachute_now()` (the menu) jumps it up and floats it down. **The cursor spun around it**: `watch_spin()` runs every tick and sums the signed angle the cursor sweeps around the pet's centre within `DIZZY_REACH` (2.6 sizes) over the last `DIZZY_WINDOW` (2 s); `DIZZY_TURNS` (2.5) in one direction is `go_dizzy()`: mood `dizzy` (spiral eyes as tube spirals, a wavy mouth, brows askew, the head lolling; `dizzy_idle_{000,060,300}`, `dizzy_squash_000`, `dizzy_stretch_000` in every sheet), nine staggering steps with the face turning, a squash, a flop, a stretch up, and a `dizzy` line, with `stars()` (an Overlay of twelve Pillow star frames circling above the head) the whole time; `DIZZY_COOLDOWN` 25 s. A slow circle or a straight pass adds nothing. It works in every visible state but in the hand, inside, hidden, behind the curtain and mid-play; it sets an errand aside and carries on after. **Dropped on the house**: `on_release` asks `house_room_at(x, y)`, which reads the house's `open_box` and `rooms` (screen boxes the open house now writes in `tell()`) or the closed house's own box, and sets `after_land`, so once the pet has landed (or floated down) it walks in with `go_inside(room)`. **Dragged out of an open room**: the house records where it drew each pet (`pet_boxes` in `draw_open`); a press on one starts a pet drag with an Overlay ghost instead of a house drag; let go outside the house it writes `plans/house-out-<pid>.json` with the drop point, `called_out()` returns it, and `come_out(at=...)` puts the pet there, floating down under the chute when it's high. Let go back over the house, nothing changes. **The bars**: a nod at 7 keys or 5 clicks in 2 s, the full reaction with the line at 15 keys in 5 s or 15 clicks in 10 s (`KeyWatch.count`, `clicks_in`; keys and clicks count anywhere on the screen).

## The hat maker
`app/hatmaker.py`, menu "Hat maker..." (and a button in the closet). Six shapes live in `MAKER` in `web/pets.js`, built with id colours: the main part red, the trim green, the sticker spot blue (half strength, and `sprite.html?flat=1` turns tone mapping off so the channels stay apart). `tools/sprites/render_maker.py` renders them as layers `outfits/maker_<shape>_sheet.png` per pet, same poses as the closet items. In the app `HM.paint()` recolours a frame by channel (the shared minimum of the channels is the shine and stays), and draws the sticker on the spot, shaded by the blue channel. Hats are `%APPDATA%\Perchlings\hats\<id>.json` (id = hash of shape, colours, sticker), worn as `wearing.hat = "my:<id>"`; every pet, the house and the stage paint them the same way. The code `PH1-<shape>-<main>-<trim>-<sticker>-<sticker colour>-<name>` is the whole hat; `from_code()` accepts it. On the web, `wear(THREE, pet, sp, "maker:cap:1E1B24:F4F1EA")` builds a shape in real colours (the look test has a Make row).

## Clips
`F.record_clip(where, seconds, fps, max_w, out_dir, name, done)`: a thread grabs the box `where()` returns each frame with `grab_region` (GDI BitBlt of just that box; PIL's ImageGrab copies the whole 4K desktop first, half a second a frame), resizes to 480 px wide, quantizes every frame to one palette and writes a GIF to Pictures/Perchlings/Clips. "Clip 8 seconds" on the pet menu records the pet on the real desktop and runs its signature move 0.7 s in; the stage's "Clip 8 s" records the stage canvas at 720 px. PIL merges identical frames, so a still pet makes a short-looking GIF that plays for the full time.

## The panel
`app/menu.py`. Every tile on every page carries a one-line tip that shows once the pointer has rested on it for 220 ms (`TIP_DELAY`): the home tiles from `TIPS` (keyed by the label, or its first word, so "Music, hears sound" is still Music), tricks and company picks from the catalog's own `what` line, play kinds from `PLAY_TIPS`, the house tiles from `HOUSE_TIPS` (the house's own panel uses the same), rooms, breaks, attitudes and the pets from `grid(tips=...)`; the Choose tricks window shows the same `what` lines on its tiles (`tile_grid(tips=...)`). One `Tip` window per toplevel does all of them: shown on Enter of the tile or anything inside it, hidden only when `winfo_containing` says the pointer has left the whole tile, its timer cancelled with the window. Until 0.28.3 `tip()` bound only the tile frame; the labels cover the frame, so Tk fired Leave the moment a hand crossed onto the label and no tip ever stayed up (a teleporting pointer, the kind a generated event makes, never crosses that edge, which is why it went unseen). The labels say what they do ("Keep you company" for the study/work/game/eat page, "Choose tricks" for the picks). The right click opens `Panel(pet, x, y)`: a borderless topmost card (closes on focus-out or Escape) with the pet's portrait, name, archetype and status line, then tiles in groups (Do; Say and share; Wear and own; Household; Settings) built by `grid()` from `(icon, label, action, more?, on?)` tuples, seven to a row. Icons are emoji drawn with the Windows emoji font (`icon()`, `embedded_color=True`, cached); tricks and Together picks show a real frame of the pose (`PREVIEW`). Sub-pages (tricks, together, play, inside, break, house, egg, attitude, pets) replace the card's body with a Back row. The house page brings the house out when there is none (`Pet.bring_out_house`), calls it over from another screen, and otherwise opens, closes, decorates, restyles, empties or puts it away by writing `household/commands/house.json` (`Pet.house_cmd`, read by the house every 300 ms); the Pets page calls a pet that is inside back out. The card lives in a canvas, so on a screen shorter than the card it scrolls with the wheel. Toggles show ": on/off" and a teal bar. Quit and Update sit in the footer. The old `tk.Menu` is gone.

## The household keeps itself whole
`instance_running(id)` opens the one-copy mutex without taking it. `stale_household()` lists pets whose `here/<pid>.json` still exists but is older than 45 s (a crash, or the updater's force-close) and whether the house's `house.json` is stale the same way; `keep_household()` relaunches those unless their lock is held. Every pet runs it 6 s after start and every 30 s (`keep_tick`). A pet that quit removed its file and a house closed by the owner removed its own, so they stay away. A start with no arguments launches every pet that isn't running and the house, and becomes the first of them.

## Pet ids, eggs and colours

A pet id is its species id, or species id plus `#n` for a hatchling (`ears#2`); `species_of()` strips the suffix, state files are named by pet id, and `adopted_ids()` lists the four kinds first then hatchlings. `app/eggs.py`: the odds table (common 60% natural colour, uncommon 30% Lilac/Mint/Peach/Sky, rare 9% Midnight/Sunset/Snow, legendary 1% Golden), `roll()`, `recolor()` (HSV: hue shifted from the species' base hue to the colour's, saturation and lightness scaled; applied to the pet's own frames in `Frames.compose`, so outfits are untouched), `egg_image()`. A pet counts a "good day" when touched 3+ times (`care` in its state); after 7 good days since `egg_baseline` it finds an egg (`household/egg.json`, one per household, at most `MAX_MINIS` = 6 minis; bought pets never count), which sits next to it (an overlay that follows it; click for the time left) and hatches 24 h later: a new state file `<species>#n` with `hatched: true`, `variant`, a name like "Peach Teal", started as its own process. A hatched pet is a mini: drawn at `E.MINI_SCALE` (72%) of its kind's size for good, `hatched_ids()` counts them, and the panel header calls it a "mini diva" or whatever its kind is. Presence carries `species` and `variant`, so the house and the stage draw the right colour.

## The streamer stage

`app/stage.py` (`Perchlings.exe --stage`, from any pet's menu). A normal window titled "Perchlings Stage" with a chroma backdrop (green, blue, magenta, or cream/night for non-keyed use) that draws every pet that is out, at its relative taskbar position, from what each pet announces (mood, pose, yaw, wearing, and the current speech bubble, which presence now carries), plus the closed house where it stands. ## The house

`web/house.js` is the house as three.js geometry (a two-storey cross-section: living room and kitchen down, bedroom and bathroom up, a staircase, a gable roof; plus a closed outside with a door and windows) and fourteen pieces of furniture, each placed in its room. `tools/sprites/house.html` + `render_house.py` render `assets/house/closed.png` (512, for the taskbar), `open.png` (1200 x 900, walls neutral), one see-through layer per piece in `furniture/`, and `layout.json` (per room: floor line, where a pet stands, how tall a pet is there, the box to paint; plus the door and ground in both views). Walls are painted in the app by multiplying the room box with the chosen colour.

`app/house.py` is its own program (`Perchlings.exe --house`, started by any pet that finds no house out, and by the "Bring out the house" tile on any pet's panel; one instance). Closed, it is a colour-keyed window on the taskbar, draggable across monitors like a pet. Its right click opens a card like the pets' (`HousePanel`: open or close, Decorate, the other style, who is inside with a portrait each to call out, Everyone out, Put it away). Decorating is pictures: `decorate_dialog(room=None)` shows a preview of the open house that redraws on every click, the two styles as tiles, and for each room its pieces as tiles (a piece cut out of its layer; on = outlined; a price on the ones in the shop) and the wall colours as chips. A click on a room in the open house opens the same dialog for that room. The pets drive the house through `household/commands/house.json` (open, close, toggle, decorate, style, out, move, quit), read in `tick()`. A click opens it: the painted open render plus the furniture that is on, then every pet whose presence file says `inside: <room>` drawn from its own sheets at that room's spot (bedroom asleep on the bed, living room sitting on the couch, kitchen eating, bathroom behind the curtain, which is drawn last). It writes `household/house.json` (door position, monitor, open) a few times a second so pets know where the door is. State in `%APPDATA%\Perchlings\house.json` (position, furniture on/off, wall colours); shop pieces are `house:<piece>` in `owned.json`.

Pets: `go_inside(room, seconds)` walks to the door then hides the window and announces the room; `come_out()` reappears at the door and walks off; a call-out (the house card, a pet's panel, the stage) writes `plans/house-out-<pet>.json`. Breaks happen in the bathroom when a house is out; a nap goes to the bedroom for 1.5 to 3 minutes at most once every 20 to 45 minutes (`next_house_nap`), so the taskbar is not empty all day; the panel has Go inside (living room, bedroom, kitchen, up to 15 minutes) and Nap, which uses the bedroom when the house is out.

The open house: a left click on a room opens the decorate dialog for that room (placed at the click; `room_at` hit-tests `LAYOUT["rooms"][*]["wall"]` down to the floor), a drag moves the whole open house along the taskbar (`open_drag`, `open_pos`), and only the right-click card (or a pet's panel) closes it. "Put it away" quits the house and removes its door file, so it stays away until a pet's panel brings it out again; a house that merely went quiet (a crash, an update) is brought back by the keeper.

## Owning things

`app/closet.json` marks what comes with every pet (`included`: beanie, party hat, glasses). `app/shop.json` lists the rest with prices, the store link, the store's license endpoint, and `variants`: a map from the store's variant id to the item ids it unlocks (filled in once the products exist). Purchases live in `%APPDATA%\Perchlings\owned.json`, shared by every pet in the household. The Shop window shows Buy buttons (open the store in the browser) and the menu has "Enter a code..." which checks the code once with the store and unlocks the items. Before the store exists, a local `test-codes.json` next to `owned.json` maps test codes to items; it is never shipped.

Households: every adopted pet comes out with the desktop icon unless it is "at home" (`home` in its state file). The Pets submenu on any pet sends others home or brings them out, and "Adopt another..." opens the adoption window (`--adopt`).

## Updates

The packaged app looks for the newest release 20 s after it starts, every minute after that (`UPDATE_EVERY`), and whenever the panel opens if the last look was more than ten seconds ago. One look serves the whole household: `look_for_update()` reads `household/update.json` (`tag`, `etag`, `ts`) and only asks GitHub when that answer is older than the minute, so five pets make one request a minute, not five. The request carries the last `ETag` as `If-None-Match`; GitHub answers an unchanged release with a 304 that does not count against the unauthenticated rate limit (60 an hour), and `newest_version()` returns `("same", etag)` so the known tag stands. No answer at all (offline, a 403) leaves the last known tag in place. Nothing goes out but the version number in the user agent. When there is a newer one, the pet says so and an "Update to x.y.z" item appears at the bottom of the menu, redrawn on the spot if the panel is open (0.28.2; before that the app looked only every six hours, so a release published while the pets were running went unseen for hours, and the menu never showed it until the next look). Worker threads never touch Tk directly: they hand the result to `Pet.later(fn)` and the tick runs it (`root.after` from a thread raises "main thread is not in main loop" outside `mainloop`). `do_update()` downloads `PerchlingsSetup.exe` into `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Perchlings-update` and runs it with `/SILENT /FORCECLOSEAPPLICATIONS /NORESTART`; the installer closes every running pet, replaces the files, and its silent-mode `[Run]` entry starts `Perchlings.exe` again, which brings out everyone who isn't at home. The per-pet `pet.txt` survives, and so do all the state files.

`site/index.html` is the whole shop site, built as a product page (2026-09-16, after the owner said the earlier rewrite was cosmetic): the hero is the live 3D lineup on a dark stage (the same `pets.js` as the app; the pets blink, breathe, follow the cursor, say their real lines in bubbles, hop and wave when they speak, and a click tries one of the free hats on them), one literal headline, one sentence, the price in the button, a trust line; then Meet the four (the meet clips), What they do (nine moments, one picture each, the true-size desktop clip first), Things they've said, The price ($1.99 card and a $0.99 add-on shelf), Getting one, five Questions, a closing ask, and a bar that follows you once the hero button scrolls away. ## The test run
`python tools/testrun.py` drives everything on the real app code in a scratch data folder (`.testrun/`, wiped when all is well): one pet in-process through every trick, together pick, hide, breaks, the sulk and the nudge, reminders, the notebook, a sign, a photo, a party, the dance, reactions, mischief, the house, eggs and hatching, the hat maker and every dialog, with every frame it asks for checked against the sheet; then the other three pets; then real pets, the house and the stage as separate programs, driven through the command files (`household/commands/<pid>.json`: tickle, wave, dance, gossip, party, out, and `play`/`trick`/`together`/`inside`/`hide`/`break`/`wear` with their fields). `quick` runs the first part only, `part2` the processes only. ## Rules

- The catalog (tricks, behaviours, gadgets, closet shelves) is research-backed; every item has a source. See `docs/catalog.md`.
- No wellness or productivity features.
- No ads, no mystery boxes, no chat, no social feed. Fixed prices.
- Local-first: what the pet learns about the owner stays on their computer.
- An adult holds the account; a child can be the owner. No birth years, location, photos or messaging.
