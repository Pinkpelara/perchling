What they do

Everything below comes with the app on day one, and all of it is in one little menu when you right-click your pet. The pictures are the real pets doing the real moves.

You decide how much trouble it makes.

There's a dial with three settings. Sweet means it behaves. Cheeky means muddy footprints across your screen and sticky notes left lying around. Menace means it steals your cursor, spins out, faints when you walk away, and lies down right where you're working. Change it whenever you want.

39 tricks.

Backflip, moonwalk, karate, robot, spin out, parkour, yoga, panic, scream, jumpscare, and 29 more. Right-click your pet, pick a trick, and it does it. It also does them on its own when it feels like it, and each of the four has a favorite.

Play music and they dance.

Put on anything, from any app. After a couple of seconds every pet on your screen starts dancing, and they all dance in step with each other. Eight moves, two seconds each. It only listens to how loud your speakers are, so it has no idea what song is on.

It can turn into a folder.

When you need the space, pick Hide and your pet becomes a plain folder. Drag the folder wherever you like. Every so often two eyes peek over the edge. Pick Come out and it hops back.

Tell it things and it remembers.

There's a notebook you can type into. Your cat's name, a test on Friday, what you'd like to be called. Later on, your pet brings it up. And if you have two or more pets, they pull up chairs and talk to each other about what you told them. All of it stays on your computer.

44 things to wear, plus a hat maker.

Crowns, beanies, sunglasses, a hoodie, a suit, headphones, angel wings, a jetpack. Before you pick one, the closet shows your pet wearing it. If none of those are right, make your own hat: you choose the shape, the colors, a sticker and a name. Your hat gets a code you can text to a friend, and their pet can wear it too.

It has a house.

A little house sits on the bar next to your pets. They walk in for a nap or a bathroom break and walk back out. If there's no house out, a curtain drops in front of them right where they are. Click the house and it opens up into four rooms. Click a room to change the walls and the furniture. There are two styles, Cozy and Loft.

They play together.

With two or more out, they chase each other, wrestle, race, dance, swap hats and march in a parade. And if you play with a pet on seven different days, it finds an egg. A day later the egg hatches into a mini, a pocket-size pet in a random color, anything from Mint to Golden, that stays small for good. You can't buy eggs, only earn them. A household can raise up to six minis on top of the pets you adopt.

It'll study, work or game with you.

Pick one from the menu and your pet pulls out a laptop, or headphones, or a snack, and settles in next to you. It stays there until you click it.

It notices how you're doing.

Type really fast and it hops up and cheers you on. Three undos in a row gets you a comment. Lock your computer and it sleeps until you're back, then says hi. It only counts key presses, never which keys, so it can't read anything you write.

It learns when you show up.

After a few days it knows what time you usually sit down, and it says hi when you're on time. Ignore it for two hours and it turns its back on you until you tickle it. Tell it your birthday and there's confetti on the day. Ask it to remind you about something and it'll hop up and tell you when it's time.

Good for clips and streams.

One click records eight seconds of your pet as a GIF you can post anywhere. If you stream, there's a green-screen window with every pet on it, and buttons to make them wave, dance, gossip or throw a party on camera.

Questions

Do I have to keep something open?

No. The pet is the app, so there's no window behind it. Right-click it and pick Quit when you want it gone for a while, and uninstall it like any other app to remove it for good.

What does it know about me?

Only what you tell it and what it sees: the notebook, your birthday if you gave it (just the day and month), what time you usually show up, what it's wearing, and how much you've played with it. That all lives in one small file on your computer. It goes online for two things, to check whether there's a newer version and to check a code you type in, and nothing about you goes with either. It hears how loud your speakers are, never what's playing.

Can I get one for my kid or my little sibling?

Sure. There's no account and no chat, and buying anything inside the pet takes a code from whoever paid, so you stay in control.

Will it get in my way?

It stays on the bar at the bottom and out of your windows. Drag it anywhere, on any screen. Pick Hide and it's a folder until you want it back. Set its attitude to Sweet and it never touches your cursor.

What about Mac? Phones?

Windows 10 and 11 for now, and Mac is next. Phones don't let anything walk on top of other apps, so we're not going to promise you one.

Will Windows warn me when I open it?

It might, for now. Until the app is in the Microsoft Store, Windows can show a warning for a new app it hasn't seen before. Click More info, then Run anyway. It installs into your own user folder, doesn't need an admin password, and shows up in Add or remove programs like anything else.

How do I add another pet later?

Same as the first one. Adopt it on the site and you get a code. Right-click any pet, choose Enter a code, and the new one walks out. Closet items and house pieces work the same way. If you get a new computer, install the app there and enter your codes again.
