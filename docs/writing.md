# Writing

House style for anything a person will read: this repo's docs, text inside the app, the shop site, the store listing, emails to owners.

## Rules

- Plain, everyday words. If a nine-year-old and a grandparent both understand it, it's right.
- One idea per sentence. Vary sentence length; don't string three short ones in a row.
- Say the specific thing. "The pet waits at the edge of the screen around the time you usually log in" beats "the pet responds to your routine."
- No dramatic reframes ("It's not a toy. It's a friend."), no punchline endings, no "Here's the thing."
- No em dashes. Use a comma, a colon, or a full stop.
- No exclamation marks in the app or on the site. The pet can be excited; the copy stays calm.
- Explain a technical word in brackets the first time it appears, then don't use it again if a plain word works.
- Names: pets are labelled by colour until they have real names. No cute-sounding invented names in copy, ever, without a decision first.
- Never claim wellness, therapy, focus, or productivity benefits. It's a pet.
- Never mention competitors' products in the app or on the site. Research stays in `docs/`.

## Before committing text

Run the humanizer pass: structural tells first, then vocabulary, then punctuation, then a hollow check (does this say something specific?). Fix what it finds. If a line reads like a slogan, cut it.

## Words we use

owner (not user), pet, adopt, name it, pick five, closet, look (a saved outfit), the menu (right-click), sulking, tickle, the shop, seasonal shelf.

## Words we don't

entitlement, inventory, SKU, engagement, retention (fine in docs, never in copy), gamified, immersive, companion experience, journey, unlock, seamless, delightful, magical.
